Firewall manager · Windows and Linux · open source

Know what your firewall will do before it does it.

StockFW drives the firewall your system already has. It shows every connection that was allowed or blocked, keeps your policy as files you can read and diff, and answers “what would happen if?” before you change a thing.

Overview

Am I protected right now?

One sentence answers it, and says for which address families. Below it: how many connections were allowed and blocked since the app opened, what is open right now and which program owns it, your adapters, and the most recent blocks.

How the Overview page works

  • IPv4 and IPv6 are reported separately. On Linux, if ip6tables is missing the page says “IPv6 is not filtered” and why, instead of a reassuring green.

  • Open connections show the owning program, read from /proc on Linux and the TCP table on Windows. A connection on a port something here listens on is marked inbound, not guessed from port numbers.

    [[verified:test-core.sh]]

  • Virtual adapters (containers, VMs, VPN) are hidden by default and counted, so a laptop with Docker does not read like a server farm.

The traffic is real: a staged network with a scanner on the outside and two services on the inside.]]

Events

What just happened?

Every connection the firewall allowed or blocked, as it happens, with the program, both addresses, the port and the service name. Blocked rows are red. Type in the filter and the list narrows as you type; the count says “3 of 1200 events” so an empty result is never mistaken for a quiet firewall.

The Events page, and turning an event into a rule

  • Right-click a row to block or allow that destination, block that service to it, or trust an inbound source. The dialog prints the exact rule before anything is written.

    [[verified:test_eventrule_builders]]

  • Rows land newest first by their own time stamp, duplicates are refused, and the list keeps 100,000 rows by default, up to a million. Turn on event history and they are saved to files only your account can read.

    [[verified:ui-hitview-smoke]]

  • On Linux the list is read straight from the kernel ring buffer, so it works on WSL, in containers and on systems without a syslog daemon.

Above the list sit the search box, the Monitor switch, Clear, Refresh, a filter bar for direction, verdict, protocol, port, address and time, Reset and Export CSV.|Blocked rows in red. The filter bar narrows by direction, verdict, protocol, port, address or CIDR block and time, and Export CSV writes what is shown.]]

Activity

How much, and when?

Two graphs over the last 15 minutes to 24 hours: allowed and blocked events per minute, and bytes per second through your adapters. A crosshair reads any point with the mouse or the arrow keys, and every graph has a table view.

Reading the Activity graphs

  • Time before the app started is shaded “no data” rather than drawn as a flat zero that would look like a quiet night.

    [[verified:fs-activity-smoke]]

  • Each event is counted at its own time, not when the app happened to read it, so a batch of history read at start-up does not become a spike.

    [[verified:fs-activity-smoke]]

  • Throughput comes from the adapters’ own byte counters. No packet capture, no trace session, no elevation.

    [[verified:test-core.sh]]
[[shot:activity|The Activity page: 15 min selected, three tiles for blocked, allowed and current throughput, then two line graphs, Policy activity and Network I/O, each with a flat line until a burst of scans and connections and a drop back to zero.|Fifteen minutes on the staged network: a few minutes of scans and connections, then quiet. The throughput tile reads zero because the traffic has just stopped.]]

Policy

What will the firewall do?

Your rules, grouped by kind, each one a row you can read. A Covers column says whether a rule reaches IPv4, IPv6 or both, and why. Nothing reaches the firewall until you press Apply, and Apply shows you the impact first.

Rule kinds, the files behind them, and Apply

  • What you save is what loads back: the policy round-trips through its files unchanged, and a row that does not parse is skipped and counted, never silently changed.

    [[verified:test-core.sh]]

  • Apply goes through one gate: it measures the change against what is live, records what it applied, and if the firewall refuses, restores the last good policy and says whether that worked.

    [[verified:test_apply_gate]]

  • Audit-only mode stages changes and writes the audit line while leaving the firewall untouched, so you can rehearse a policy.

    [[verified:test_apply_gate]]
[[shot:policy|The Policy page: cards for “Allow connections from host”, “Allow inbound service” and “Forward service”, each a table with a Covers column reading IPv4 only, IPv6 only or IPv4 + IPv6.|Each rule says what it covers. The IPv6 range covers IPv6 only; “Anyone” covers both families.]]

Copilot

What should I do about it?

Fixed checks over the event log and your policy: port scans, repeated login attempts, timed contacts, fan-out, blocklist hits and the first time a program steps outside its habits. Every finding opens a drawer with the events behind it and the numbers next to the limits. A policy health check finds duplicate, shadowed, unreachable and risky rules and offers only fixes What-If has verified.

Findings, policy health and auto-block

  • No model, no network. The Copilot is a deterministic parser and a set of counters. It works on an air-gapped machine and cannot be prompt-injected because there is no prompt.

    [[verified:test-core.sh]]

  • Auto-block can only ever tighten the policy, for ten minutes, with one-click undo, and never for your own network, multicast, loopback or a destination the program already used regularly.

    [[verified:test-adversarial.sh]]

  • Every block and fix prints the literal rule and waits for your Yes, and “Changes made by the Copilot” lists each one with Undo. There is no “apply all”.

    [[verified:test-health.c]]
[[shot:copilot|The Copilot page: Sentinel set to Detect + auto-block, tiles for Needs attention, Policy health, Auto-blocks active and Services learned, and three findings: a Critical port scan, Critical repeated login attempts and Info repeated SSH attempts.|Three findings after a port scan and two rounds of login attempts. None of them stages a rule by itself: Sentinel never changes inbound rules.]]

Trust

Can I trust this program?

Inspect any file: its SHA-256, size and date, and on Windows whether it is signed and by whom. Attest the ones you trust; the baseline tells you later if one of them changed. Scan the running processes in one click.

Attesting programs and reading the baseline

  • Signature checks use WinVerifyTrust offline. No revocation lookups, no network, no hangs.

    [[verified:test_trust_engine]] [[win]]

  • Hashing runs on a worker thread, one file at a time, so a two-hundred-megabyte installer never freezes the window.

    [[verified:fs-trustview-smoke]]

  • Every attest and forget is an append-only line in the audit log, next to the policy changes.

    [[verified:test_audit_log]]
[[shot:trust|The Trust page: a path box with Inspect, Refresh list and Scan processes buttons, a report for /usr/bin/nc.openbsd with its SHA-256, size, modification time and “Matches the attested baseline”, and a baseline list of three programs, curl, nc.openbsd and python3.13, each marked in sync.|Three attested programs, all in sync with their baseline. On Linux there is no code signature to read, so Publisher says “no signer” rather than guessing.]]

Design rules

What StockFW refuses to be.

A firewall tool is a privilege amplifier: whatever it can be tricked into applying, it applies with your rights. So the architecture is defined as much by what it leaves out as by what it does.

NeverA kernel driverStockFW never touches a packet. It configures the filtering engine your operating system already runs and reads the log that engine already writes. There is no driver to crash, sign or exploit, and if StockFW stops, your firewall does not.
NeverA cloud, an account, a modelThe Copilot is a parser and a set of counters that run on your machine. Nothing about your rules or your traffic leaves it. It works with the network cable unplugged.
[[verified:no network client in the source tree]]
NeverSilent changesNo file watcher, no auto-apply by default, no background service. The firewall moves when a human presses Apply in a window that has the right to do it, and every attempt is one line in the audit log.
[[verified:test_apply_gate]]
NeverRules that open more than you approvedSuggestions are allow-only and print the exact rule. Auto-block is deny-only, time-boxed to ten minutes, and cannot touch rules you wrote yourself.
[[verified:test_sentinel_auto_gates]]
NeverShell commandsEvery tool StockFW runs, netsh, iptables, wevtutil, is started with an argument list, never a command line. A rule name cannot become a second parameter.
[[verified:append_spawn_arg, the exact inverse of CommandLineToArgvW]]
AlwaysYour own toolsThe policy is text. git diff it, copy it to back it up, read it in Notepad. Everything StockFW knows, wf.msc, nft list ruleset and your event viewer can confirm.

The same engine, from a terminal.

stockfw drives the same policy files, simulator and apply gate as the desktop app, so a rule added in a script is the rule the Policy page shows. It says whether what you saved is live, follows events with a status line, shows who can reach each open port, and reads the audit log. New in {{version}}

What 1.0.7 adds.

The terminal release: on Linux, stockfw does from a terminal what the window does, settings included, and says what is true. The changelog has the full list, and the command line page covers each command with its real output.

The live event line

stockfw events -f prints new events in the table’s columns with a status line under them: firewall state, live or pending, blocked today, Sentinel and the event source. Pipes and --json get events and nothing else.

Is what I saved live?

stockfw status says “9 rules, live” or “1 change not applied”, the last apply, the blocks today and whether the boot service puts the rules back. policy diff shows the difference; rules have references such as inbound-allow-service:2.

Who can reach this port?

stockfw listening lists every open port, the program behind it and whether the policy lets the LAN and the internet reach it, with the rule that decides.

Settings and rules, edited from the terminal

stockfw config reads and sets the Preferences with the window’s own checks; policy edit changes a rule in place; --apply saves and applies under one lock. Boot uses the settings last applied.

The Copilot, Sentinel and Trust, from the terminal

stockfw copilot scan finds what the Copilot page finds and copilot block puts a verified block live alone; sentinel lift takes a block down; events act turns an event into a rule; trust check says whether an attested file changed. Each asks first, or takes --yes in a script.

The audit log and the guide, from the terminal

stockfw audit reads every apply, start and stop with filters and follow mode; stockfw help topics prints the Help window’s 25 topics, also installed as man stockfw-topics.

The lockout guard

Over SSH, apply and start check that the new policy still lets your session in, under sudo too and with sshd on any port. They ask on a terminal and refuse in a script unless you pass --yes.

Events triage

events --summary counts a window per source and per port with a bar per hour; --wide, --csv, port lists and ISO times. The RULE column names the blocklist that blocked an event.

Honest options and tables

A value that starts with a dash is a value, an unknown option is refused, a typo gets a suggestion, tables fit the terminal and only the terminal, and text from logs is escaped before it reaches your screen.

man stockfw and completion

A manual page written by the tool from its own command tables, and bash and zsh completion that offers commands, options, values and your own rule references.

Wayland and WSL

No tray icon is attempted where GTK has none, so the start-up warning is gone, a background start shows the window, and the window keeps up with a firewall stopped from a terminal.

Since 1.0.7

What 1.0.7 brought.

The previous release rebuilt the desktop app; all of it is in {{version}} too.

IPv6 on Linux

One policy loaded into both families in one step, rolled back together if either half is refused. Neighbour discovery, router adverts and DHCPv6 replies always pass.

The redesigned desktop app

A sidebar, an Overview that answers “am I protected?” in one sentence, and every page rebuilt as cards. The screenshots on this site are of it.

The Copilot, reworked

Fixed checks with the evidence shown, a policy health check with verified fixes, and a list of every change it made with Undo. No model, no network, and it says so.

Threat-intel blocklists

Opt in to well-known feeds such as Spamhaus DROP and abuse.ch Feodo Tracker, on Windows and Linux. StockFW downloads, checks and loads them; on Linux events say which list blocked a connection. Off until you turn it on.

Country and network-owner blocking

Block by registered country or by the network that announces an address, from an offline, signed location database. The picker warns about anycast networks.

Background mode and sign-in

Keep protecting after the window closes, start hidden at sign-in, and on Linux let the boot service put the rules back before the network comes up. One copy per user.

Events at volume, with history

100,000 events in the list, a filter bar, CSV export, and an optional history on disk that only your account can read and the command line can search.

Help and About

A searchable Help window that opens with F1 at the page you are on and works offline, and an About window with the credits, the licence summary and Copy system info.

Preferences redesigned

Seven pages in two groups, a line on what each is for, and a mark on every row that will change before you press Save.
